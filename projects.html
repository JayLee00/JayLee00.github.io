DexFIT — Force Informed Transfer for Dexterous Manipulation

A 16-DoF wearable glove whose joint structure is mechanically identical to the target robot hand. Because the kinematics match one to one, retargeting disappears from the pipeline. Fingertip tactile readings drive a Force Generation module that adds supplementary joint flexion, so the robot squeezes in proportion to the operator's own pressure.

At deployment a vision-free Diffusion Policy runs on proprioceptive data alone, while an Adaptive Force Control module watches for stalls and compensates for rising friction. The full system reaches 100% success on in-hand light bulb rotation, 34.5% faster and with 16% less energy than position-only isomorphic control.

Isomorphic Tactile Glove
16-DoF wearable glove, four fingers by four joints, with rotary potentiometer encoders at 12-bit resolution and three fingertip tactile modules. Angle tracking reaches R² = 0.998 with 2.59° RMSE; the whole device weighs about 180 g in PLA and Onyx nylon composite.

TACOS Glove
Tactile Augmented Control & Optimization System. Twenty-one barometric taxels on flexible PCB across three fingertips under 3 mm Ecoflex-50, with temperature-drift compensation by least squares. Force repeatability RMSE 0.064 N over a 0–6 N range, response under 50 ms. Firmware through serial receiver to Kalman filtering and visualisation.

16-DoF robot hand carrying 60 barometric taxels at 250 Hz, twelve three-axis kinesthetic force/torque sensors at 300 Hz and sixteen magnetic encoders, all over EtherCAT at 1 kHz. Tactile sensitivity reaches 1.5 mN.

Whole-Upper-Body Teleoperation
Vive trackers drive both arms while the glove drives the hands. Tracker deltas from an engage anchor become absolute end-effector targets across the network; glove joints map one to one with ramping, exponential smoothing and rate limits. A three-pedal foot switch handles stop, go and logging.

Two pipelines turning RGB-D into executable grasps for the FR3 and its four-finger hand.
